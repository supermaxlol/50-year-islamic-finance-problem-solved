# Minsky: cashflow validation, not accounting alone

Minsky's [1992 Financial Instability Hypothesis](https://levyinstitute.org/pubs/wp74.pdf)
distinguishes units whose operating cash covers all contractual payments
(hedge), those that must refinance principal (speculative), and those whose
operating cash cannot cover interest as well as principal (Ponzi). “Ponzi” here
is a financing category, not necessarily fraud. Prosperity can encourage a shift
toward fragile balance sheets; refinancing pressure and asset sales can amplify
declines. Fraud or concealed accounts are not necessary to that mechanism.

The connection to this programme is due-date affordability: Stage 207 checks
cash at every supplied event, and Stage 209 requires construction plus that
buffer before release. They address a specified funding mismatch; they do not
prove Minsky's economy-wide cycle or eliminate it.

A zero-interest principal obligation can still be impossible to refinance.
Ownership funds can still suffer operating losses, valuation collapse or
liquidity pressure. Transparent records help make obligations inspectable;
they cannot create missing cash or guarantee reliable evidence.

The current main mathematical manuscript and supplement do **not** cite Minsky
(checked 3 November 2026). His work is relevant to broader financing/liquidity
motivation, not a premise of the current valuation proof. This code release
adds this contextual note without changing that manuscript.
